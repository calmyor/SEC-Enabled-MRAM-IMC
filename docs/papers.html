From accuracy limits to an SEC-enabled silicon system.

The publication sequence begins with a model-based question about computational accuracy, demonstrates statistical error compensation in a measured 22 nm MRAM IMC macro, and then connects the behavioral model, circuit architecture, fixed-point implementation, measurement protocol, and application result in the journal account.

QuestionHow do array scaling and parasitic attenuation limit the signal quality of resistive in-memory computation?
Silicon answerOffset-compensating sensing and learned SEC factors recover signal quality inside a fabricated 22 nm MRAM IMC macro.
Full accountThe journal paper links the error model, α/γ/θ correction, fixed-point hardware, compute-SNDR method, and measured outcomes.

Conference paper

Compute SNR-Boosted 22 nm MRAM-Based In-Memory Computing Macro Using Statistical Error Compensation

Saion K. Roy, Han-Mo Ou, Mostafa G. Ahmed, Peter Deaville, Bonan Zhang, Naveen Verma, Pavan K. Hanumolu, and Naresh R. Shanbhag.

The compact conference account establishes the scaling problem, offset-compensating sensing, low-overhead SEC, measured 2.7–6 dB SNR improvement, the 5× iso-SNR energy trade-off, and the final fully connected layer CIFAR-10 result.

Reading key

Four labels separate the layers of evidence.

Silicon

Fabricated macro

ADC-column SNDR, SEC improvement, energy/SNDR points, fabricated overhead, and final-layer accuracy.

Circuit simulation

OCCS behavior

Transistor-level mismatch, PVT, Monte Carlo bitline variation, and sensing-area comparison.

Behavioral model

Array and fixed point

Parasitic transfer behavior, α extraction, quantized convergence, and precision selection.

Projection

Storage alternative

SEC area changes from 12.2% in the fabricated implementation to 3.7% with SRAM-backed storage.

TerminologyThe conference paper reports SNR; the journal uses SNDR to include distortion. SEC means statistical error compensation, and its training learns hardware correction factors.
